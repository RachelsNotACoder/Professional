The "Screening to Lease" journey targeted users who signed up to run a one-time background check, a transactional segment with 95% within-year dormancy and Rent Payments adoption under 5%. The journey was underperforming against holdout.

The existing sequence followed landlord workflow logic (screen → lease → move-in → collect rent), but that assumed users planned to stay. Most didn't. The first email promoted a $75 lease feature, asking a cold, transactional user to pay again immediately after a free screening report.

Snagajob had no way to see the overall revenue performance of lifecycle programs, only open and click rates on individual emails. One series, daily job recommendations, absorbed roughly 80% of send volume on the assumption that more sends meant more results, with no data to check it.

I built a UTM tagging framework from scratch, then used it to build an Adobe Analytics dashboard connecting each email program to downstream outcomes: site visits, applications, and revenue. Programs were grouped into four themes (job recommendations, worker engagement, onboarding, and transactional), so performance could be compared across them for the first time.

The dashboard immediately surfaced a misallocation: pure merchandising emails were high-volume and frequent cadence with poor overall ROI, while engagement emails were low-volume but consistently strong. Shifting send volume toward engagement and onboarding, while scaling back merchandising frequency and targeted audience, drove 31% revenue growth and 6.7% visit growth, while cutting total send volume by 22%.

6.7% visit growth · 22% reduction in send volume · Adobe Analytics · UTM framework · Snagajob

Every year, a significant percentage of new HSA account holders failed Patriot Act identity verification, a regulatory requirement that prevented accounts from being funded. Conduent's only outreach was a paper mail notice: dry, legalistic, and delivered to a physical address that was often undeliverable and, in the majority of cases, the reason the account had failed in the first place. Response rates were around 5%.

I built the first lifecycle email program targeting this segment. Automated a data trigger to identify employees in failure state and route them into a personalized sequence. Messaging led with consequences specific to each employee's situation: employer-funded account holders were told they were missing money their employer was trying to give them; self-funded holders were told they were losing the tax benefits of having an HSA. Required actions were personalized to the specific failure type. Four automated follow-ups for non-responders. Measured against a paper-mail holdout at launch.

Automated trigger sequence · ~4,000 employees annually · Conduent

Snagajob's marketing had always focused on the employer side. No one had studied why workers actually searched, applied, or disengaged. The working assumption was simply "they want a job," which didn't inform messaging or segmentation.

I fed hundreds of open-ended worker survey verbatims into ChatGPT, prompting it to surface behavioral and motivational patterns rather than polished profiles. Four segments emerged with high accuracy, including students sending money home, seniors seeking low-stress part-time work, and "maximizers" stacking multiple jobs. I validated and pressure-tested the groupings against my own judgment before packaging them for Marketing and Product.

TurboTenant already had a freemium-to-Premium sequence triggered by behavioral signal, clicking a Premium upgrade prompt in-product, not just dismissing it. The trigger logic was sound. What it lacked was any personalization: every user who clicked got the same generic pitch they'd already seen and failed to act on.

I rebuilt the sequence to personalize using business-level attributes (property count, landlord tenure, self-reported priorities) while keeping the existing behavioral trigger in place. Email one led with the single most relevant feature for that user. Email two broadened to the full value prop. Email three offered a discount.

Tested against the existing flow in a 50/50 split. The personalized sequence converted at 30%, a 15% lift over control, confirming that matching the pitch to the user beats a generic one, even before the discount comes in.

The previous year's version of this campaign tied its goal to subscription retention directly, which meant it could only speak to users who already had strong numbers. Anyone whose year looked thin got left out, reducing the program's reach, and it wasn't making a case for deepening the landlord's relationship with the platform. It was specifically missing an opportunity to demonstrate value to less active users

I reframed the goal around feature adoption instead. That let the campaign include low-activity users by turning weak numbers into opportunities: a landlord with zero rent reminders sent saw that stat next to copy explaining what automating reminders would do for them. Personalization pulled from rent collected, features adopted, portfolio growth, tenant applications, maintenance requests, and Premium status.

Measured against a 15% random holdout, with non-openers tracked separately. Result: 55% open rate, 4% CTR, and a 0.5% lift in two-month retention against holdout, plus incremental adoption gains on the specific features the campaign called out.

I combined in-product signals and stakeholder interviews with worker survey data to identify which behaviors and expectations mattered most to new signups and which in-product actions were worth building toward, arriving at the personas and personalization opportunities that would shape the series. Snagajob's emailed job recommendations were filtered by zip code only, on the assumption that hourly workers had high lateral job mobility and little preference for job type. I tested that assumption instead of accepting it.

Persona research and AI-assisted analysis of worker behavior showed the opposite: workers had strong preferences for job types similar to roles they'd recently applied for. I built a job type taxonomy grounded in BLS categories, refined it against Snagajob's own application data, and filtered recommendations to match each worker's recent application history. Built and launched in about two months against a control group.

20% more return visits than control · taxonomy built from BLS categories · Snagajob

In yield optimization on unsecured revolving credit at Wells Fargo, CX was fielding steady complaints about interest rates, customers comparing them to mortgages and home equity loans. The team's proposed fix was to give reps more room to lower rates, which would have created a real revenue and margin problem on rates that were already priced correctly.

The actual issue was that customers were comparing the wrong products. Anyone upset about a revolving credit rate who also owned a home was often a legitimate candidate for a home equity product at a genuinely lower rate. I reframed the complaint as a qualification signal instead of an objection to resolve, and wrote a script giving reps a clean redirect: acknowledge the frustration, then ask whether a home equity option might fit better. Worked with supervisors to roll it out to the team.

Turned a rate objection into a repeatable cross-sell motion for CX · kept the revolving credit book intact · Wells Fargo
