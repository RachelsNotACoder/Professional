Situation

During a migration of the core Rent Payments retargeting flow from Klaviyo to Customer.io, I recognized the existing flow was several years old and built on a fear/loss-aversion messaging model tied to a landlord persona that no longer matched our broader acquisition mix.

Task

Use the migration as an opportunity to validate whether the messaging still resonated — and increase conversion of "RP Clickers" (users who showed intent but didn't complete setup) by updating the value prop to align with current landlord mental models.

Action

I hypothesized that landlords didn't recognize themselves in the old messaging — they viewed rent collection as a solved problem, even if their actual "solution" was manual and tedious. Version B dropped the fear framing entirely and focused on removing drudgery: manual reminders, chasing late payments, writing receipts. I emphasized that the feature was free and saved hours of admin work. I built an A/B path test within the migration itself to validate the hypothesis.

Result

The automation-framed path delivered a 72% lift in Rent Payment adoption. Rent Payments is a primary ARR driver via tenant fees and the platform's stickiest retention feature — creating a recurring monthly engagement loop with landlords.

Situation

TurboTenant's onboarding series for incomplete signups hadn't been updated in some time, despite meaningful changes in the acquisition mix. For two specific intent segments, the series wasn't beating holdout — meaning the emails were producing no measurable lift over doing nothing.

Task

Redesign the incomplete-signup onboarding series for users who signed up to find tenants ("Marketing" intent) and users who signed up to collect rent ("Rent Payment" intent) — to drive property-add completion and clear holdout performance.

Action

I diagnosed portfolio size as the primary gap: all messaging had been written for small landlords, systematically ignoring larger landlords who behaved differently — lower open rates but significantly higher conversion when they did open, and entirely different value propositions. I rebuilt the series as a single journey per intent segment, using Liquid scripting to serve differentiated content blocks by property count. For larger landlords, I surfaced the Payments Dashboard and optimized subject lines to lift open rates. For smaller landlords, messaging stayed benefit-forward at their scale. A/B tested old vs. new.

Result

+11% relative lift in property-add completion for Marketing intent signups and +5% lift for Rent Payment intent signups — both clearing holdout performance the original series had never reached. Durable learning: larger landlords convert at a disproportionate rate when they open.

Situation

TurboTenant's transactional email infrastructure was fragmented across SendGrid, Klaviyo, and Intercom — each owned by a different team. SendGrid, controlled by Engineering, made personalization structurally difficult: static templates, limited dynamic content, and any change requiring a dev ticket. When TurboTenant launched a managed property service, the scope of personalized transactional communication required made the fragmentation untenable.

Task

Migrate transactional email ownership from Engineering/SendGrid into Marketing/Customer.io, consolidating the martech stack and enabling the personalization and testing capabilities the business needed to scale.

Action

Led the full migration across multiple product teams — building Trello-based per-team workflows, joining Sprint Demos to stay ahead of product changes, and creating documentation to build cross-team buy-in. Rebuilt emails in Customer.io using Liquid logic and dynamic content blocks so a single template could surface the right content per user situation. Simultaneously unlocked structural A/B testing that SendGrid's architecture had blocked.

Result

Delivered 30 net-new transactional emails in 6 weeks at product launch — with 3–4 content revisions per email as procedures evolved — at a pace Engineering couldn't have matched mid-release. Testing cadence accelerated roughly fourfold. Established Marketing as the owner of transactional messaging, removing a persistent dev dependency and enabling channel expansion to SMS and push.

Situation

TurboTenant had 8 million email addresses accumulated over 10 years, but only ~100–200K landlords were actively using the platform. The CEO's Chief of Staff pushed to blast a promotional partner offer to the largest possible audience — with an attempt to pull rank rather than discuss tradeoffs.

Task

Push back on an executive-level request in a way that would actually land — protect email deliverability and sender reputation without turning it into a political fight.

Action

I pulled cohort data from Metabase and Klaviyo and built a chart showing the correlation between user recency and adverse engagement rates. I connected that to deliverability mechanics: a spike in spam reports triggers inbox providers — particularly Google — to downgrade sender reputation for everyone, including active users. The case reframed the ask from "how big can we go" to "what does this cost us permanently." The CEO sided with the data. That organizational buy-in then enabled broader deliverability reforms: sunsetting disengaged users, tightening the newsletter blast, establishing best practices for promotional GTM sends.

Result

Partner send scoped to users active in the last 90 days. Broader reforms drove unsubscribe rate from 0.355% to 0.213% — a ~40% improvement — during a period of high new signup volume.

Situation

TurboTenant's "Screening to Lease" journey targeted users who signed up to run a one-time background check — a transactional, low-intent segment with 95% within-year dormancy. The journey was underperforming against holdout and Rent Payments adoption was under 5%.

Task

Diagnose why the journey was failing and redesign it to drive meaningful feature adoption in a segment that hadn't come to TurboTenant for an ongoing relationship.

Action

Audited the existing sequence and identified two structural problems: the email order followed landlord workflow logic that assumed users planned to stay — most didn't — and the first email promoted a $75 lease feature, asking a cold user to pay again immediately. I reframed the strategic goal: instead of walking users through a landlord checklist, the journey needed to reposition TurboTenant as worth an ongoing relationship. I rebuilt the sequence to lead with Rent Payments — free for landlords, recurring by nature, and the feature most likely to create a monthly visit cadence.

Result

Rent Payments adoption rose from under 5% to 40% — an eightfold increase — measured pre/post against the redesigned journey across ~200 users per week.

Situation

TurboTenant had an existing post-modal email sequence targeting freemium users who engaged with Premium upgrade prompts in-product, but conversion performance left room to improve.

Task

Redesign the sequence to convert more high-intent freemium users into paying subscribers by making the value prop more relevant and the cadence more deliberate.

Action

Built a 3-email sequence over 10 days, triggered by behavioral signal (in-product modal clicks — not dismissals) and personalized using business-level attributes: property count, landlord tenure, and self-reported feature priorities. Email 1 led with the single most relevant Premium feature for that user. Email 2 gave the full value prop. Email 3 offered a discount. Tested 50/50 against the existing flow.

Result

The redesigned sequence achieved a 30% conversion rate — a 15% lift over the control — confirming that leading with personalized, attribute-driven value props outperformed a generic premium pitch.

Situation

TurboTenant's prior year-in-review campaign had retention as its primary goal — but that framing had two structural problems: it limited the eligible audience to users with strong numbers, and a single email untimed to renewal has limited retention impact on its own.

Task

Redesign the campaign with a goal that could reach a broader audience and drive measurable behavior — not just goodwill.

Action

Reframed the strategic goal around feature adoption — unlocking users with low or zero activity. Instead of hiding weak stats, the campaign surfaced them as opportunities: a landlord with zero rent reminders sent saw that stat alongside copy explaining that Rent Payments would automate reminders so they'd never chase rent again. Personalization pulled from rent collected, features adopted vs. not adopted, portfolio growth, maintenance requests, and Premium status. Measured against a 15% random holdout, with non-openers tracked separately.

Result

55% open rate, 4% CTR, 0.5% lift in 2-month subscriber retention against holdout — with incremental feature adoption gains across targeted features.

Situation

Snagajob had Adobe Analytics connected to lifecycle email, but inconsistent event structure meant visit and revenue data couldn't be reliably monitored. Performance decisions were being made on opens and CTRs — not business outcomes. One series alone accounted for ~80% of send volume and was operating largely as spray-and-pray.

Task

Fix the measurement layer so lifecycle performance could be evaluated against real business outcomes, then use that visibility to optimize.

Action

Standardized Adobe Analytics event structure, classified all email programs into four themes (daily job recommendations, worker engagement, welcome/onboarding, transactional), and built a reporting framework capturing downstream performance: site visits, job views, applications, and direct session revenue via last-touch attribution. Validated against historical data to control for seasonality. Reliable baseline within six weeks.

Result

The report immediately surfaced a critical misallocation. Shifting resources toward engagement and onboarding — and scaling back job recommendation frequency — drove 31% revenue growth, 6.7% visit growth, and 22% reduction in send volume. Efficiency and outcomes improved simultaneously.

Situation

Snagajob's emailed job recommendations were filtered by zip code only. The prevailing assumption was that hourly workers had high lateral mobility and low job-type preference — recommendations were broad by design.

Task

Test whether that assumption was correct — and if not, build something better.

Action

Used persona research and AI tools to investigate hourly worker job-seeking behavior. Found meaningful evidence that workers had strong preferences for job types similar to roles they'd recently applied for — lateral mobility was lower than assumed. Built a de novo job type taxonomy grounded in BLS categories and refined against Snagajob's application data, then filtered recommendations to match each worker's recent application history. Built and launched in approximately two months against a control group.

Result

20% more return visits than the control group — validating both the behavioral insight and the filtering approach.

Situation

Running lifecycle at Snagajob meant producing high-quality content continuously against ~45MM sends/week. My scope then expanded to include all worker-facing blog and social content — roughly quadrupling my production workload. Early AI use surfaced an immediate problem: outputs varied widely in voice, tone, and format, and required heavy manual revision before anything could go out.

Task

Build a repeatable AI-assisted content system that produced on-brand output across email, blog, and social without manual style correction on every piece.

Action

Selected three top-performing worker blog posts that cleared both a quantitative bar (SEO) and a qualitative one (brand voice). Fed them to Jasper and prompted it to identify the underlying voice and style patterns — not to produce output, but to surface the rules embedded in the writing. Repeated the exercise with emails to capture register differences between formats. Embedded the resulting parameters into my standard prompt setup so voice, tone, and format handled themselves while individual prompts focused on topic and concept.

Result

Content scaled across three formats without adding headcount. One email produced through this system drove 70% more return visits than baseline — a clean causal read. The AI-generated personas became a core input to the team's segmentation framework — a lasting change to how the team operated.

Situation

Every year, a significant percentage of new HSA account holders failed Patriot Act verification — a regulatory requirement preventing accounts from being funded without a confirmed physical address. Conduent's only outreach was a paper mail notice: dry, legalistic, delivered to an address that was sometimes the source of the problem. Response rates were around 5%.

Task

Build the first lifecycle email program targeting this segment — employees who had an unfunded HSA but hadn't taken the steps to fix it, often because they didn't understand what was wrong, why it mattered, or what to do.

Action

Automated a data trigger to identify employees in Patriot Act failure state and route them into a personalized email sequence. Messaging led with consequences relevant to each employee's situation: employer-funded account holders were told they were missing out on money their employer was trying to give them; self-funded holders were told they were losing HSA tax benefits. Required actions were personalized to the specific failure type. Included four automated follow-ups for non-responders. Rewrote tone from legalistic to value-forward. Measured against a paper-mail holdout at launch.

Result

Activation rate rose from 5% to 27% — a fivefold increase — across approximately 4,000 employees annually.

Situation

TurboTenant's Product team used Twilio to send non-promotional SMS — campaigns outside Marketing's ownership and largely unmonitored. No one had visibility into total send volume or deliverability. I needed this data to plan a migration to a new sending architecture routing messages through Customer.io before Twilio execution. Twilio is a developer-first platform with no native marketer-facing reporting; pulling the data required API access and a custom script.

Task

Get a working picture of Twilio SMS performance quickly enough to inform active planning decisions, without pulling BI resources onto a low-urgency exploratory request.

Action

Using Cursor and Gemini, I wrote and ran a Python script against Twilio's API using credentials I already had access to. Iterated in Cursor until the script produced the output I needed. One hour of active work, two hours of background execution, yielding a CSV I could import directly into a spreadsheet.

Result

Completed in an afternoon what would have taken a minimum of two weeks in the BI queue — and produced data that had apparently never existed for this team: a clear picture of non-promotional SMS volume and deliverability that we could use to sequence and prioritize the migration work.
