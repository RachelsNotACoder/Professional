TurboTenant

A/B testing

Rent Payment activation reframe

An aging fear-based retargeting flow hadn't been touched in years — and the landlord profile it was written for no longer matched the acquisition mix. I rebuilt the messaging around automation value, removing the loss-framing entirely, and A/B tested the hypothesis inside a platform migration.

+72%adoption lift

A/B tested messaging reframe · Customer.io

Read the full story

During a migration of the core Rent Payments retargeting flow from Klaviyo to Customer.io, I recognized the existing flow was several years old and built on a fear/loss-aversion messaging model tied to a landlord persona that no longer matched our broader acquisition mix.

Use the migration as an opportunity to validate whether the messaging still resonated — and increase conversion of "RP Clickers" (users who showed intent but didn't complete setup) by updating the value prop to align with current landlord mental models.

I hypothesized that landlords didn't recognize themselves in the old messaging — they viewed rent collection as a solved problem, even if their actual "solution" was manual and tedious. Version B dropped the fear framing entirely and focused on removing drudgery: manual reminders, chasing late payments, writing receipts. I emphasized that the feature was free and saved hours of admin work. I built an A/B path test within the migration itself to validate the hypothesis.

The automation-framed path delivered a 72% lift in Rent Payment adoption. Rent Payments is a primary ARR driver via tenant fees and the platform's stickiest retention feature — creating a recurring monthly engagement loop with landlords.

Snagajob

Analytics

Measurement-first analytics rebuild

Lifecycle programs were tracked at the email level only — no visibility into visits, applications, or revenue. I standardized the Adobe Analytics event structure, built a reporting framework from scratch, then used that visibility to diagnose a high-volume series operating as spray-and-pray and cut it back.

31%revenue growth

22% reduction in send volume · Adobe Analytics

Read the full story

Snagajob had Adobe Analytics connected to lifecycle email, but inconsistent event structure meant visit and revenue data couldn't be reliably monitored. Performance decisions were being made on opens and CTRs — not business outcomes. One series alone accounted for ~80% of send volume and was operating largely as spray-and-pray.

Fix the measurement layer so lifecycle performance could be evaluated against real business outcomes, then use that visibility to optimize.

Standardized Adobe Analytics event structure, classified all email programs into four themes (daily job recommendations, worker engagement, welcome/onboarding, transactional), and built a reporting framework capturing downstream performance: site visits, job views, applications, and direct session revenue via last-touch attribution. Validated against historical data to control for seasonality. Reliable baseline within six weeks.

The report immediately surfaced a critical misallocation. Shifting resources toward engagement and onboarding — and scaling back job recommendation frequency — drove 31% revenue growth, 6.7% visit growth, and 22% reduction in send volume. Efficiency and outcomes improved simultaneously.

Conduent

0-to-1 program build

Patriot Act activation program

Employees failing identity verification couldn't fund their HSA accounts. The only outreach was a paper notice most people ignored. I built the first lifecycle email program targeting this segment — personalized by failure type, consequence, and employer funding status — against a paper-mail holdout.

5→27%activation rate

De novo program · ~4,000 employees annually

Read the full story

Every year, a significant percentage of new HSA account holders failed Patriot Act verification — a regulatory requirement preventing accounts from being funded without a confirmed physical address. Conduent's only outreach was a paper mail notice: dry, legalistic, delivered to an address that was sometimes the source of the problem. Response rates were around 5%.

Build the first lifecycle email program targeting this segment — employees who had an unfunded HSA but hadn't taken the steps to fix it, often because they didn't understand what was wrong, why it mattered, or what to do.

Automated a data trigger to identify employees in Patriot Act failure state and route them into a personalized email sequence. Messaging led with consequences relevant to each employee's situation: employer-funded account holders were told they were missing out on money their employer was trying to give them; self-funded holders were told they were losing HSA tax benefits. Required actions were personalized to the specific failure type. Included four automated follow-ups for non-responders. Rewrote tone from legalistic to value-forward. Measured against a paper-mail holdout at launch.

Activation rate rose from 5% to 27% — a fivefold increase — across approximately 4,000 employees annually.
